# Panorama Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Ship a one-row Panorama tool in both apps. Live captures automatically, guided by the gyroscope. Studio picks frames from a slow-pan video. Both stitch with OpenCV.js in a worker and save a JPEG through Share… / Save file.

**Architecture:**
- Pure-JS measurement and geometry modules live in `core/stitch/`: thumbnails, sharpness, phase-correlation shift, capture rules, frame picker, camera model, and the 3×3 rotation and cylinder maths. They have no DOM or OpenCV dependency.
- A module worker (`core/stitch/stitch-worker.js`) loads the vendored OpenCV.js from bytes the page has already validated. It matches neighbouring photos as they arrive, then places, projects, gain-compensates, blends, crops and encodes them.
- `core/stitch/stitcher.js` is the page's client for that worker.
- The two pages, `live/panorama/` and `recorded/panorama/`, connect frame sources to those pieces.

**Tech Stack:** Vanilla JS (ES2022 modules, module workers), OpenCV.js 4.10 (`@techstark/opencv-js@4.10.0-release.1`), WebCodecs (existing `FileVideoSource` and `Mp4Encoder`), OffscreenCanvas, DeviceOrientation/DeviceMotion, Cache Storage, Web Crypto (SHA-256).

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-04-panorama-design.md`. It builds on `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-03-live-recorded-yolo-design.md`.

## Global Constraints

- **No build step:**
  - no bundler, no npm, no build step;
  - no root-absolute URLs: use relative imports, and `new URL(rel, import.meta.url)` for fetched assets, because the site is served from the GitHub Pages sub-path `/sightline/`;
  - libraries are vendored in `shared/vendor/` and imported only through `shared/lib/` wrappers, never from a CDN at runtime.
- **OpenCV.js:**
  - source: `https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@techstark/opencv-js@4.10.0-release.1/dist/opencv.js`;
  - vendored to `shared/vendor/opencv/opencv.js`;
  - its byte size and SHA-256 are recorded in `shared/vendor/VERSIONS.md`, and in `OPENCV_ASSET` (Task 2);
  - **downloading it needs the user's explicit approval in chat first** (Task 3, Step 1).
- **Lenses:** horizontal field of view on the long side: Wide **67°**, Ultra Wide **106°**. `focalPx = (longSide / 2) / tan(hfov / 2) × zoom`.
- **Live capture:**
  - a photo is due at yaw step **0.65 × displayed hfov**;
  - shoot only if the rotation rate is **< 30°/s**, pitch and roll are within **8°** of Start, and sharpness is **≥ 0.8 × the median of the last 15 frames**;
  - "Slow down" above **40°/s**;
  - "Go back a little" when the yaw since the last shot is over **0.9 × hfov**;
  - the direction is set after **5°** of turn;
  - auto Done at **12** photos.
- **Studio picker:**
  - targets at **k × 0.65 W**, with a window of **± 0.1 W**;
  - the direction is set at **0.1 W**;
  - the final pick needs **≥ 0.3 W** of movement after the last pick;
  - "Pan was fast" when a per-frame shift exceeds **0.15 W**;
  - at most **12** photos;
  - total movement **< 0.5 W** shows "Not enough panning".
- **Matching:**
  - ORB on a grey copy **800 px** on its long side, with **2000** features;
  - Hamming kNN with a **0.75** ratio test;
  - RANSAC at **3 px**, needing **≥ 30** inliers;
  - in Live, a gyro disagreement of **> 5°** counts as a failure;
  - focal refinement needs **≥ 3** matched pairs and is accepted within **±25%** of the table value.
- **Output:**
  - cylindrical projection;
  - area capped at **16,000,000 px**, with uniform downscaling;
  - JPEG quality **0.9**.
- **User-facing text, copied exactly:**
  - "Slow down", "Keep level", "Go back a little"
  - "Not enough turning", "Not enough panning", "Pan was fast", "Used the first 12 frames of the pan"
  - "Panorama needs motion access. Reload the page and tap Allow."
  - "One seam was placed from the gyroscope and may be visible"
  - "Stitched frames 1–N; frame M didn't match." (N and M filled in)
  - "The panorama was lost when the app was closed."
- **Existing conventions:**
  - every `localStorage` access is wrapped in try/catch;
  - the footer reads `RESEARCH DEMO · NOT A DRIVING AID`;
  - app names are **Sightline Live** (`live/`) and **Sightline Studio** (`recorded/`).
- Commit messages end with `Co-Authored-By: Claude Opus 5.5 <noreply@anthropic.com>`. Commit locally; never push.

**Running tests (used by every task):**

2. **Show the browser pane.** Video and rendering tests time out while it is hidden.
3. Navigate the pane to `http://localhost:8000/tests/?grep=<pattern>`. The pattern matches a test name or a file name.

## Review Focus

1. **Phone held landscape in Live.** Capture spacing must use the displayed width's field of view, which is 67° when landscape and about 41° when upright. Pinned by `camera-model: displayed hfov follows orientation` (Task 5).
2. **Auto-exposure changing across a pan.** iOS brightens and darkens as you turn, and seams must not show as steps. Pinned by `stitcher: alternating brightness still blends` (Task 10).
3. **Turning back the other way mid-capture.** Reversing must never trigger shots over photos already taken. Pinned by `capture: reversing does not shoot` (Task 6).
4. **A right-to-left pan in Studio.** The picker must space frames the same in either direction. Pinned by `select: leftward pan picks the same spacing` (Task 7).
5. **Portrait-sized frames**, such as an upright iPhone clip: the focal length must come from the long side, whatever the orientation. Pinned by `camera-model: portrait uses long side` (Task 5).

**Files:**

**Interfaces:**
- Produces:
  - `toThumb(image: CanvasImageSource, maxWidth = 256) → { data: Float32Array, width, height, scale }`:
    - a grey (luma) copy whose width is `min(maxWidth, imageWidth)`, keeping the aspect ratio;
    - `scale = thumbWidth / imageWidth`;
    - it uses an OffscreenCanvas.
  - `sharpness(thumb) → number`: the variance of the 4-neighbour Laplacian over the interior pixels.
  - `phaseShift(a, b) → { dx, dy, peak }`:
    - it gives the shift of `b`'s content relative to `a`, in thumbnail pixels; a positive `dx` means the content moved right;
    - both inputs are zero-padded to the next power of two, after a Hann window over the content;
    - it uses a 2D FFT (radix-2, in the file), the normalised cross-power spectrum, an inverse FFT, the arg-max, and a 3-point parabolic sub-pixel fit on each axis;
    - `peak` is the correlation peak value, between 0 and 1.

### Task 5: Camera model and geometry

**Files:**
- Create: `core/stitch/camera-model.js`, `core/stitch/geometry.js`
- Test: `tests/stitch-geometry.test.js`

**Conventions, stated once and used everywhere:**
- Camera axes: x right, y down, z forward. Matrices are row-major arrays of 9 numbers.
- An absolute rotation `R` maps camera rays to world rays.
- **Yaw** is positive when turning right, about the world's y axis. **Pitch** is positive when looking up. **Roll** is about the view axis.
- For neighbours i and j, with H mapping image-i pixels to image-j pixels: `Rij = K⁻¹ H K`, made orthonormal, and `R_j = R_i · Rijᵀ`. Photo 0 has `R = I`.

**Interfaces:**
- Produces from `camera-model.js`:
  - `LENS_HFOV = { wide: 67, ultrawide: 106 }`
  - `focalPx({ lens, width, height, zoom = 1 }) → number`, computed from the long side.
  - `displayedHfov({ lens, width, height, zoom = 1 }) → degrees`: the field of view along `width` as displayed. It is 67° for a 16:9 Wide frame shown landscape, and `2·atan((short/long)·tan(33.5°))` ≈ 41° shown upright, divided through by zoom using the tangent, not linearly.
- Produces from `geometry.js`:
  - `rotX(deg)`, `rotY(deg)`, `rotZ(deg)`, `mul(a, b)`, `transpose(a)`, `apply(R, v)`
  - `intrinsics(f, cx, cy) → K`
  - `nearestRotation(M) → R`: the 3×3 polar decomposition, via Jacobi SVD written in the file.
  - `rotationFromHomography(H, f, cx, cy) → Rij`
  - `yawPitchRoll(R) → { yaw, pitch, roll }`, in degrees.
  - `chain(rijList) → R[]`, which has length `rijList.length + 1`.
  - `waveCorrect(R[]) → R[]`, horizontal:
    1. Take the covariance of the cameras' x-axes (first columns of R).
    2. Its smallest eigenvector is the up vector, with its sign chosen so it agrees with the average camera −y.
    3. Rotate every R so that up becomes world −y.
  - `focalFromHomographies(H[], width, height) → number | null`: OpenCV's `focalsFromHomography` formula, giving the median of the valid per-pair estimates, or null.
  - `cylProject(R, f, x, y, cx, cy) → [u, v]`, the forward map, and `cylToImage(R, f, u, v, cx, cy) → [x, y] | null`, the inverse map. With `θ = atan2(d.x, d.z)` and `h = d.y / hypot(d.x, d.z)`, `u = f·θ` and `v = f·h`. The inverse returns null when the ray is behind the camera.
  - `cylBounds(R[], f, width, height) → { uMin, uMax, vMin, vMax }`, from sampling each photo's border, 32 points per edge.
  - `outputScale(bounds, maxPixels = 16e6) → number`, which is at most 1.
  - `cropRect(colTop: Float32Array, colBottom: Float32Array) → { x, y, width, height }`:
    - it trims leading and trailing columns that have `colTop > colBottom`;
    - `y` is the maximum of `colTop` and `y + height` the minimum of `colBottom`, over the remaining columns.
